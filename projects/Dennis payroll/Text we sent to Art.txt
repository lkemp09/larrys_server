Yes, Lyman. Your friend is remembering the rule correctly: Alaska’s paid sick-leave law took effect July 1, 2025, and covered employees generally earn one hour of paid sick leave for every 30 hours worked. Employers with fewer than 15 employees may cap annual accrual at 40 hours; employers with 15 or more may cap it at 56 hours. An existing PTO plan can satisfy the requirement when it meets the law’s accrual and use rules.

For pricing purposes, that works out to a maximum direct labor cost of:

1 ÷ 30 = 3.33% of wages

So, for a $30-per-hour employee:

Sick-leave wage cost is about $1.00 for every hour worked
With a 15% payroll burden, it becomes about $1.15 per hour
That is the amount the customer billing rate would need to rise just to recover the estimated cost, assuming all accrued sick leave is eventually used

I entered the posted 2026 Local 1547 journeyman wireman wage of $57.44 per hour as an example.

The important limitation is that there is no single IBEW payroll-burden percentage for every Alaska electrical company. Alaska Electrical Trust Funds says contribution rates vary according to the particular collective bargaining agreement or special agreement. Health, pension, retirement savings, training and other amounts may be hourly or percentage-based, depending on that agreement.

The calculator therefore leaves the actual union contribution amounts open for him to enter from his current monthly remittance form or CBA. The federal defaults include 6.2% employer Social Security and 1.45% Medicare. Alaska’s 2026 unemployment wage base is $54,200, and the standard employer rate shown by the state is 1.50%, although his company’s assigned experience rate may differ.

The most useful next document would be his current IBEW/NECA contribution-rate sheet or collective bargaining agreement. With that, we can enter the exact numbers instead of estimates.
